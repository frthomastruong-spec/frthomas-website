Sunday Homily

Neither Rolling Inward nor Lying Flat — Only Faithfulness

33rd Sunday in Ordinary Time · Sunday, November 15, 2026

33rd Sunday in Ordinary Time — Year A

Readings: Prv 31:10-13, 19-20, 30-31 · Ps 128 · 1 Thes 5:1-6 · Mt 25:14-30

Dear brothers and sisters,

Young people in China today use two popular expressions to describe the pressure of modern life. I think these two expressions help us understand today's Gospel.

The first is 內卷 — nèijuǎn in Mandarin, noi6 gyun2 in Cantonese — “involution,” rolling inward.

It describes a race with no finish line. Everyone studies harder, works longer, and competes more fiercely than the person next to them — and yet nobody really moves forward. Life does not become better; people only become more tired, more anxious, and more envious. The wheel turns faster and faster, but it turns inward.

The second expression is the reaction to the first: 躺平 — tǎngpíng, tong2 ping4 — “lying flat.”

After rolling inward for too long, a person gives up. Why try, if trying changes nothing? Why dream, if every dream costs too much? So he lies flat. He does the minimum. He buries his hope.

Brothers and sisters, in today's Gospel, Jesus shows us a third way — neither 內卷 nor 躺平. Faithfulness.

A man going on a journey entrusts his property to his servants: five talents, two talents, one talent — and notice the important words of Jesus: “to each according to his ability” (Mt 25:15). The master does not give everyone the same amount, because he does not expect everyone to produce the same result.

The first two servants go and trade, and they double what they received. But listen carefully: the master says exactly the same words to both of them. To the one who made five more talents, and to the one who made only two more, he says: “Well done, good and faithful servant. Come, share your master's joy” (Mt 25:21, 23).

The same praise. The same joy. The same invitation.

This is the Good News that breaks the logic of 內卷. In the Kingdom of God, we are not ranked against each other. God does not ask me, “Why are you not like your brother?” He asks me, “Have you been faithful with what I entrusted to you?” One person receives five talents of ability, opportunity, health, or time. Another receives two. Another, perhaps, only one. Heaven is not a competition. The question is not how much we have compared with others — the question is whether what God gave us has been loved, used, and shared.

But then comes the third servant — and he is the man who chose 躺平.

He digs a hole and buries his master's money. He does not steal it. He does not waste it. He simply does nothing with it. And when the master returns, we hear the real reason: “Master, I knew you were a demanding person… so out of fear I went off and buried your talent in the ground” (Mt 25:24-25).

Fear came first. Lying flat came second.

Because he believed his master was hard and unjust, he did not dare to risk, to love, or to try. And this, brothers and sisters, is a warning for us. When we imagine God only as a demanding judge waiting for us to fail, we will bury everything He gives us. We will bury our time, instead of offering it. We will bury our faith, instead of sharing it. We will bury our capacity to forgive, to serve, and to love, because we are afraid. A buried talent does not disappear — but it bears no fruit, and it brings joy to no one.

Saint Paul, in the second reading, tells us we are not people of the night or of darkness; we are “children of the light and children of the day” (1 Thes 5:5). Children of the light do not live in the anxiety of 內卷, always comparing, always afraid of falling behind. And children of the day do not live 躺平, asleep while the Lord is coming. They stay awake. They stay sober. They work quietly, peacefully, faithfully — day after day.

And look at the woman praised in the first reading, from the Book of Proverbs. She works with willing hands, she rises to care for her household, and — this is the sign that her work is blessed and not frantic — “she opens her hand to the poor, and reaches out her hands to the needy” (Prv 31:20). A person trapped in 內卷 has no time for the poor; he is too busy competing. A person 躺平 has nothing left to give; he has buried his gift. But a faithful person works — and because his heart is at peace, his hand is open.

So today, let each of us ask honestly before the Lord: Where am I?

Am I rolling inward — comparing myself with others, restless, exhausted, as if my worth depended on how much more I can produce? Lord, free me from that race. Teach me that I am Your child before I am anything I achieve.

Or am I lying flat — burying a gift because I am tired, afraid, or disappointed? Perhaps I have stopped praying as I used to. Perhaps I have a word of reconciliation I have not spoken, a service in the parish I have avoided, a person in need I keep passing by. Lord, free me from that fear. Remind me that You are not a hard master, but a Father who rejoices over every small, faithful step.

The master in the Gospel will return, and we do not know the day or the hour. But we do not wait for him in anxiety, and we do not wait for him asleep. We wait for him the way the faithful servants did: using what we have received, in trust, for love.

Brothers and sisters, let me leave you with one sentence:

唔好內卷,唔好躺平,要忠心。
M4 hou2 noi6 gyun2, m4 hou2 tong2 ping4, jiu3 zung1 sam1.
Do not roll inward. Do not lie flat. Be faithful.

And one day, may each of us — with five talents, with two, or with only one — hear those beautiful words from the Lord Himself:

“Well done, good and faithful servant. Come, share your master's joy.”

Amen.

Fr. Thomas
